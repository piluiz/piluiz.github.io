## How it works

1. **Sign in and pick classes:** students create an account and enter the courses they want to take.
2. **Fetch every section:** the Java backend pulls each course's lectures, labs, discussions, and quizzes from USC's Schedule of Classes.
3. **Find every valid schedule:** the backend tries every combination of sections and keeps the ones with no time conflicts.
4. **Pick the best one:** valid schedules are ranked by average RateMyProfessor score, or by walking distance between buildings.
5. **Save and share:** students save their schedules and can add friends to see theirs.

## Checking for conflicts with bitsets

The scheduler checks a lot of combinations, so conflict checks needed to be fast. Each section becomes a bitmap of the week: Monday through Friday, 7am to 10pm, one bit per 10 minutes, for 450 bits. Two sections conflict exactly when their bitmaps overlap, so each check is a single AND instead of comparing start and end times.

## My part

I built the pages people actually use: signing up and logging in, entering classes, viewing generated schedules, and finding friends to see theirs. Behind those pages, I worked on the MySQL database that stores accounts, saved schedules, and friend lists.

A lot of my work was integration: making the PHP frontend, the database, and the Java scheduling servlet work together as one app. With six people each building their own piece, the hardest part was often getting those pieces to talk to each other.

## Takeaways

This was my first full-stack project, and it was cool to see every layer come together, from the database and Java backend to the pages people actually click through. Working on a team of six also meant splitting the app into pieces that could be built separately and still fit together at the end.

The biggest thing we'd improve is speed. Trying every combination of sections grows quickly as you add classes, so larger schedules took a long time to generate.
